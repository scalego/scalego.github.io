When do you use each form?

A comparative compares two people or things: "My car is faster than yours." Use than to introduce the second one.

A superlative picks the top member of a group: "It is the fastest of them all." Use the before the adjective; use in or of to name the group.

tall → taller than → the tallest

Short adjectives: -er / -est

Most one-syllable adjectives (fast, old, long) take -er for the comparative and the -est for the superlative.

fast → faster than → the fastest

Spelling: three details

1. If a short adjective ends in consonant + vowel + consonant, double the last letter: big → bigger, wet → wetter.

2. If it ends in -e, add only -r or -st: nice → nicer, safe → the safest.

3. If it ends in consonant + y, change y to i: easy → easier, funny → the funniest.

Long adjectives: more / the most

Many longer adjectives stay the same: put more before the comparative and the most before the superlative.

popular → more popular than → the most popular
difficult → more difficult than → the most difficult

Irregular adjectives: learn the forms

These common adjectives do not follow the usual pattern:

good → better → the best
bad → worse → the worst
far → further → the furthest
little → less → the least
many / much → more → the most

Common mistakes

1. Do not combine the two patterns: "more taller" is wrong. Use -er or more, not both.

2. Use the with the superlative in these exercises: "the fastest," not "fastest" on its own.

3. Use than after a comparative, not "that" or "then": "slower than a bus."
